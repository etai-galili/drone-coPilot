DJI AVATA 2 — ELECTRICAL AND POWER SYSTEM

INTELLIGENT FLIGHT BATTERY (VERIFIED SPECS)
- Capacity: 2150 mAh.
- Nominal voltage: 14.76 V.
- Energy: 31.7 Wh.
- Chemistry: LiPo 4S (four cells in series).
- Weight: approximately 145 g.
- Provides up to approximately 23 minutes of flight (forward flight, windless, sea level).

The battery is a smart battery: it reports charge level, cell health, temperature, and cycle count to the aircraft and goggles. It performs self-discharge to a safe storage level after a set idle period.

CHARGING
- Charge with the DJI USB-C charger or the Two-Way Charging Hub (Fly More Combo).
- The Charging Hub can store multiple batteries and, in power-accumulation mode, transfer remaining charge from lower batteries into the highest one.
- Charge within the recommended temperature range; do not charge a hot battery straight after flight.
- A full charge from near-empty takes on the order of tens of minutes with the standard charger; using the hub to charge several batteries is sequential.

BATTERY LED INDICATORS (ON THE BATTERY / AIRCRAFT)
- Four LEDs show charge level in roughly 25% steps.
- During charging, LEDs pulse to indicate progress; all solid means full.
- Blinking patterns can indicate temperature protection or a fault — stop use and let it reach normal temperature.

AIRCRAFT STATUS LED (VERIFIED INTERPRETATION)
- Solid Green: ready to fly.
- Blinking Yellow: GNSS/compass calibration or acquiring positioning.
- Blinking Red: low battery warning — land soon.
- Alternating Red/Yellow: IMU or sensor error — do not fly.
- Solid Blue: connecting / initializing.

POWER MANAGEMENT AND WARNINGS
- Low Battery Warning: the goggles prompt the operator to return; plan to land with reserve.
- Critical Low Battery: the aircraft auto-lands at the current position if it cannot reach Home.
- Smart RTH on Battery: the system estimates whether remaining charge is enough to return and can trigger RTH automatically.
- Cold weather reduces available capacity; warm batteries before flight in low temperatures.

ELECTRICAL CARE AND TROUBLESHOOTING
- Keep contacts clean and dry; corrosion or moisture can cause connection faults.
- A battery that will not power on may be in deep-sleep storage state — connect to a charger briefly to wake it.
- A battery that drains abnormally fast, runs hot, or shows swelling must be retired and recycled safely.
- Track cycle count; capacity and runtime decline as cells age.
