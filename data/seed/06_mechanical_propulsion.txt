DJI AVATA 2 — MECHANICAL AND PROPULSION SYSTEM

AIRFRAME
- Integrated cinewhoop design with ducted propeller guards molded into the frame.
- The ducts surround all four propellers, protecting them and bystanders and allowing safe operation near obstacles, indoors, and in confined spaces.
- Compact dimensions: 185 x 212 x 64 mm; takeoff weight approximately 377 g.
- Lightweight composite/plastic shell housing the flight controller, sensors, camera, and battery bay.

PROPULSION
- Four brushless motors, one per duct, in a quadrotor layout.
- Quick-release propellers seated inside the ducts; each motor/propeller pair is matched to a rotation direction (CW/CCW) and must be installed in the correct position.
- Propellers are a wear item: inspect before every flight and replace in matched sets.

VISION AND SENSING HARDWARE
- Downward binocular vision sensors plus an infrared sensing system for low-altitude positioning, hover stability, and assisted landing.
- These support Normal Mode positioning and braking; the Avata 2 does not have forward/side obstacle sensors, so it relies on the ducts and pilot skill for horizontal obstacle protection.

GIMBAL MECHANISM
- Single-axis mechanical tilt gimbal carrying the camera, supplemented by electronic stabilization.
- A removable gimbal guard protects the camera during transport and handling; it must be removed before flight.

PROPELLER REPLACEMENT PROCEDURE
1. Power off the aircraft and remove the battery.
2. Identify the propeller position and its rotation marking.
3. Use the included screwdriver to remove the retaining screw.
4. Lift out the damaged propeller and seat the matching replacement (correct rotation for that arm).
5. Tighten the screw firmly but do not overtighten.
6. Verify the propeller spins freely inside the duct without rubbing.

MECHANICAL MAINTENANCE
- After each flight, inspect ducts for cracks and propellers for chips or bent blades.
- Keep the airframe free of grit; debris in the ducts can strike propellers.
- Check that the battery bay latch engages firmly so the battery cannot dislodge in flight.
- Verify motor bearings are smooth — grinding, wobble, or unusual noise indicates a worn motor.
- Do not fly with a damaged duct; a compromised duct reduces protection and can foul a propeller.

COMMON MECHANICAL FAULTS
- Propeller imbalance/damage: causes vibration, shaky video, and reduced efficiency — replace the set.
- Motor wobble or grinding: worn bearing or foreign object; service or replace the motor.
- Cracked duct: replace the affected component before flying.
- Loose battery: clean the bay and confirm the latch clicks fully home.
